## (b) Discrepancies and what was done

1. **Sample organization, brokers.** His sentence: "Open a 96-person workplace with planted departments, a broker, and Slack-style communication." Fact: `SAMPLE_SPEC` (bridge-dependent workplace, 96 people, seed 1) plants six brokers (ground truth `bridges.brokers` has 6 entries) across four departments. Used at first: "... planted departments, six brokers, and Slack-style communication." The card was then removed by the landing text, which has no sample card; the sample was not changed and still loads from "Explore the sample" in every analysis view's empty state.
2. **Before and after test.** His sentence: "The paired permutation test evaluates whether the observed change is larger than expected under reassignment of the paired values." Fact: `compareBeforeAfter` (`src/analysis/time.js`) runs a randomization test on the events: every event of the two equal periods is randomly reassigned to before or after, the networks are rebuilt, and the mean per-person change is recomputed. It deliberately does not reassign or sign-flip the paired per-person values (that test was rejected as miscalibrated, 14% false positives). Used: "The permutation test evaluates whether the observed change is larger than expected under random reassignment of the events in the two periods to before and after." (`src/ui/views/time.js:473`).
3. **Shift detection, "adjacent periods".** His sentence: "Shift detection compares adjacent periods and identifies changes that are large relative to the variability estimated from the series." Fact: each window is compared with the median and MAD of the (by default 8) windows before it, not with one adjacent period. Used: "Shift detection compares each period with the periods immediately before it and identifies changes that are large relative to the variability estimated from the series." (`src/ui/views/time.js:423`).
4. **Names excluded from content text.** His sentence: "Words corresponding to people in the dataset are also excluded from the text used for these analyses." (placed under Tone). Fact: name words are removed from the corpus used by keywords, topics and diffusion (`corpus()` with `nameStopwords`), but tone scores the cleaned message text with names in it (`affect.js` uses `cleanText` only). Used: "Words corresponding to people in the dataset are also excluded from the text used for keyword, topic, and diffusion analyses." (`src/ui/views/content.js:153`).
5. **Project file, "analysis state".** His sentence: "Save the current project to preserve the data, construction settings, and analysis state for later work in Org Signal." Fact: `projectText` saves the dataset and construction settings only; opening it recomputes the network and measures, but analyses run in the views (random-network comparisons, rank stability, content, time) and the methods log are not saved. Used: "Save the current project to preserve the data and construction settings for later work in Org Signal." followed by "Opening it here or in Data restores the same network and measures." (`src/ui/views/methods.js:270`).
6. **Learn Basics, "Nodes".** His sentence: "Nodes, ties, direction, weight, paths, and communities." Fact: the Basics section holds tie, directed, weight, path, communities and community vs planted group; there is no node concept in the glossary. Used: "Ties, direction, weight, paths, and communities." (`src/ui/views/learn/concepts.js:13`). Adding a `node` glossary entry would make his sentence true; left for his decision.
7. **Generated-data diffusion note and recovery-check settings.** Not false, but placement: his recovery paragraph says the check uses "the construction settings shown here"; the settings are therefore shown on their own line ("Construction settings: those in use when the check ran (ties from ...)") between his two paragraphs (`src/ui/generate/index.js:357`).
8. **Two-mode Network intro.** His Network intro says the view shows "comparisons with degree-preserving random networks". Fact: on two-mode data the random-network comparison is replaced by the reason it does not apply. Used: his full intro on one-mode networks; on two-mode networks the comparison sentence is left out and the selection sentence reads "Select a node to inspect its neighborhood or a tie to inspect the observations that produced it." (`src/ui/views/network.js:195-197`).
9. **Interpretation summary titles.** His headings "Comparison with random networks", "Dependence on highly central people", "Rank stability" and "Group structure" are used as the visible section headings where a section heading exists (fragility, rank stability, Groups); the disclosures themselves read "Interpretation", as the shared grammar says. People's ranking note uses his "Interpretation of a ranking". The Network comparison has no separate heading of its own; its note sits under the comparison rows and reads "Interpretation".
10. **Citation.** His citation ends at "[Software]." The repository URL that followed it was removed from the Learn citation; the next line links the repository. The README citation keeps its URL and now capitalizes "Browser-based" to match.
